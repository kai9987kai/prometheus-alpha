Prometheus-α · a regenerating-worm laboratory

Does the body remember what its lost head learned?

A worm of 32 cells grows from one founder cell. It is taught that one odour predicts a shock, and the lesson is held only in its cells' state, because every cell runs the same fixed rule. Only head tissue can smell. Cut the head off, let it regrow, and ask the new head.

What is being asked

Planarian flatworms trained to find food in a lit, rough-floored dish still show the memory after their heads are cut off and regrown (Shomrat & Levin 2013). Nobody knows where that memory waits while the brain is gone. Here the question is asked of a synthetic body where every cell's state can be read, copied and transplanted.

Two kinds of rule

E rules were trained to learn and to regenerate, but never asked to remember through a regeneration. S rules were also asked to remember after one head amputation. Trunk fragments, repeated amputation, chimeras and voltage transplants are never trained for either.

The controls

Every paired worm has an unpaired twin: the same odours, the same shocks at the same moments of its life, the same random update noise, but the shocks fall between the odours. The memory index is measured within one worm, so a change in baseline cancels.
